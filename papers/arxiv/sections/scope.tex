\section{Resource Model and Assumptions}
\label{sec:scope}

\subsection{Representations and physical memory}
An execution profile $\pi$ specifies the model, arithmetic, placement, inputs,
state lifetime, and output-quality criterion. The evaluated profile performs
text prefill and autoregressive decoding with resident KV state. A weight
representation $r$ includes its encoded payload, scales, decoding metadata,
expansion, and required copies. Checkpoint size $S_{\mathrm{ckpt}}$, complete
compact-weight size $W_{\mathrm{compact}}$, and full execution-weight footprint
$W_{\mathrm{full}}(r)$ describe different objects. None is the complete
inference working set.

Let $\mathcal D_{\mathrm{phys}}$ contain disjoint physical memory pools, with
available capacity $B_d$ in pool $d$. Aggregate capacity is
\begin{equation}
 B_{\mathrm{mem}}=\sum_{d\in\mathcal D_{\mathrm{phys}}}B_d.
 \label{eq:physical-capacity}
\end{equation}
Discrete host and device memory can be separate pools; two aliases of unified
physical memory cannot. Backing files add storage, not resident-memory
capacity. Per-pool accessibility and placement constraints still apply when
the aggregate capacity is sufficient.

The physical working set $M_d(t)$ includes attributable weights, expansion,
activations, KV and recurrent state, staging, outstanding transfers, retained
buffers, metadata, allocator and driver reserves, and resident file-backed
pages. Shared allocations count once. The feasibility condition and a sampled
observation are distinct:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 M_d^{\mathrm{peak}}&=\sup_{t\in\mathcal T}M_d(t)\leq B_d,\\
 \widehat M_d^{\mathrm{max}}&=\max_{t_i\in\mathcal T}\widehat M_d(t_i),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:working-set}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal T$ covers the declared execution interval. Samples may miss
transients or include other consumers. Independent component maxima cannot
be added to obtain a simultaneous peak.

A full execution representation cannot reside in aggregate memory if
$W_{\mathrm{full}}(r)>B_{\mathrm{mem}}$. This does not prove that storage is
necessary: complete compact weights might still fit with online expansion.
Conversely, a deliberately small cache can exercise bounded residency even
when the compact checkpoint fits in physical memory. This paper studies that
controlled-budget problem rather than compact-residency impossibility.

\subsection{Accounting and stage-local admission}
An accounting domain $a\in\mathcal D_{\mathrm{acct}}$ may count bytes, slots,
pages, or queue entries. Its configured limit $b_a$ need not cover an entire
physical pool. Accounting domains can overlap and cannot generally be added.
For an additional claim $c_a$, the capacity condition is
\begin{equation}
 U_a(t)+c_a\leq b_a\qquad\text{for every covered domain }a.
 \label{eq:admission}
\end{equation}
Already held grants are not charged again on a revisit. The owner also
validates units, overflow, source and placement compatibility, reservation
authority, and demand policy. With reserve $0\leq\rho_a\leq b_a$, the
policy-accessible ceiling is
\begin{equation}
 b_a^{\mathrm{eff}}(\delta)=
 \begin{cases}
 b_a,&\delta=\mathsf{Required},\\
 b_a-\rho_a,&\delta\in\{\mathsf{Warmup},\mathsf{Prefetch}\}.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:execution-reserve}
\end{equation}
Required execution may use the reserve, but not exceed the hard limit.

Admission returns one of three abstract outcomes. \textsf{Rejected} means
that the plan is invalid or a stage exceeds its policy ceiling even without
competing occupancy. \textsf{Wait} means that an otherwise feasible stage is
blocked by current occupancy; it retains existing grants and registers a
revisit condition. \textsf{Admitted} means that the authoritative owner has
validated and acquired the stage's claims. Admission is local to a stage,
not an atomic reservation of all future resources.

A frozen plan $P=(K,\boldsymbol q,\boldsymbol\ell)$ binds the full resource
key, read/upload/install claims, and persistent residency claims. The key
includes model instance, source identity, semantic content, encoding, backend,
device, and generations $g_s,g_x$. The destination authority supplies the
actual reservation and reuse generation before hard admission. Extent
alignment and decoding can make source, staged, and execution byte counts
different. The plan must account for those differences rather than assuming
one byte count for every stage.

\subsection{Completion, quality, and progress assumptions}
Protocol correctness requires exact identity, authorized publication, and
no reuse while physical references remain. Numerical quality is a separate
contract: visible text, generated IDs, full-vocabulary logits, routes, and
internal states provide different evidence. Argmax agreement alone does not
establish numerical agreement, and checkpoint quantization does not specify
activation or KV arithmetic.

Completion evidence $e$ must identify operation $o$, key $K$, submitted stage
$s$, both generations, outcome-specific bytes, and any required fence. A
mismatched event has no mutation or release authority. A matching event that
reports non-success can end its named stage but cannot authorize publication.
Producer success and consumer retirement also differ: finishing an upload
does not establish that a later kernel has stopped using the destination.

We use \emph{custody} to mean responsibility for retaining the physical
resources and evidence needed to close an obligation. \textsf{Unknown}
denotes an obligation whose physical completion cannot be proved. Such an
obligation retains allocations and charges, potentially indefinitely.
Cancellation changes demand or caller outcome; it is not itself a completion
certificate.

The reference contracts assume authoritative owners, source and destination
generations that identify reuse, and completion evidence supplied by the
relevant provider. Conditional progress additionally requires feasible
resources, acyclic waits, fair revisits, live providers, and eventually
observable completion. The model does not assume that a blocking provider
becomes nonblocking when called by a worker. It also does not place OS page
cache, storage-server cache, or unaccounted allocator overhead under the
runtime's logical credit limits.
