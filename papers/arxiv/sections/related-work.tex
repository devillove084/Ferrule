\section{Related Work and Positioning}
\label{sec:related}

Storage-backed and offloaded inference predate \system{}. The comparison here
is by resource representation, physical domains, quality contract, and measured
traffic---not by model name or an engine's feature list.

\paragraph{Storage-backed inference.}
FlexGen~\cite{sheng2023flexgen} places tensors across GPU, CPU, and disk and
optimizes throughput-oriented schedules with compression and attention-cache
management. DeepSpeed ZeRO-Inference's technical report~\cite{deepspeed2022zeroinference}
describes CPU/NVMe weight hosting, layer streaming, prefetch, and release.
PowerInfer-2~\cite{xue2024powerinfer2} studies smartphone inference beyond
resident capacity with neuron clusters, segmented caching, and an
I/O/computation pipeline. These systems establish that storage-backed model
inference and transfer/computation scheduling are prior art. Their existence
also rules out a first-storage-inference claim for \system{}.

The relevant distinction is not whether a GPU cache is small. A comparison
must say whether complete compact encoded weights and metadata can remain
resident, whether expanded execution weights are streamed, which physical
pools are disjoint, and whether request-time reads are physical backing reads
or cache-served logical reads. A configured cache budget or an expanded-weight
overflow does not establish storage necessity.

\paragraph{Resident-domain offload and serving.}
PowerInfer~\cite{song2023powerinfer} uses activation locality to place hot
neurons on a GPU and compute cold neurons on a CPU. This is a GPU--CPU hybrid
and should not be conflated with PowerInfer-2's storage pipeline. Host/device
movement, page migration, and file-backed I/O are different observables;
shared physical pools must not be added twice. vLLM's PagedAttention
\cite{kwon2023pagedattention} addresses serving KV memory and is a useful
resident-state comparison, but KV paging is not request-time weight
materialization. The frozen vLLM 0.28 resident observations in this paper are
supplementary and arithmetic/resource-budget non-equivalent, although both
selected crossblock engines use PP1/TP2; they are not a speedup proof.
We make no capability-absence claim about other serving systems whose primary
sources and exact profiles were not audited here.

\paragraph{Lifecycle and scheduling comparison.}
The design question in RQ3 is narrower than ``which system is asynchronous?''
The comparison requires submission/completion separation, dependency-ready
work, progress ownership, bounded queues, and completion-driven physical
lifetime. An asynchronous API does not prove event-driven progress, and
worker offload does not prove nonblocking execution. Measured
storage/transfer/compute overlap requires correlated provider or hardware
traces. The safe blocking reference needed for this comparison is not yet
implemented in the TP1 path.

\paragraph{Positioning.}
\system{} contributes a profile-scoped contract connecting source-bound plans,
stage-local admission, exact generations, physical custody, publication, and
retirement. This is a protocol and measurement position, not a claim that
prior systems lack equivalent mechanisms; their custody and failure semantics
were not exhaustively audited here. The concrete difference remains an
empirical question: whether a selected integrated profile can complete a
quality-accepted workload under declared per-domain working-set limits, what
physical traffic it causes, and whether its safety contracts survive faults.
Registry coverage, OS-cache hard caps, durable ACID, cross-rank atomicity,
NCCL/device-collective defaults, multimodal integration, and performance
leadership are not implied by the design.
