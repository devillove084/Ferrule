\section{Limitations and Remaining Evidence Gates}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Capacity and physical attribution.}
The core beyond-physical-memory claim is not established. Configured budgets
cover selected ledgers, not a total process/machine working set. Sampled
device/PSS/RSS maxima can miss transients and cannot provide complete
allocation attribution. Physical host capacity, file-cache residency, swap,
co-tenants, and the complete compact-residency alternative require separate
measurement. A shared pool counts once, while required physical copies count
at their actual locations. Discrete CUDA records do not validate shared or
unified-memory deployment.

\paragraph{Logical demand and quality.}
TP1 \texttt{-02} demonstrates application-level logical demand during four
HTTP successes in one DEBUG exploratory run. Its 36,240,064,128 reader bytes
are not physical NAS/network or block-device traffic; uploads are not cache
misses or bus measurements. Only three visible-text/usage reference canaries
passed; Hello2 was HTTP/usage only, generated IDs were absent, and complete
numerical/task quality is unestablished. DEBUG/observer cost is unmeasured.
Separate TP2 numerical test receipts cannot qualify TP1 or the resident
vLLM comparison, and the strict W8A16 failure is not waived by argmax matches.

\paragraph{Safety, integration, and progress.}
The invariants and algorithms specify intended behavior at named boundaries,
not universal integration or formal verification. The general registry is
provider-installed and distinct from prepared-KV and specialized host-KV
paths. OS page cache is outside hard-credit control. Final TP1 resource
closure is unknown and GPU quiescence remains unverified despite descendant
drain and idle checks. Unknown physical-owner custody can permanently reduce
available capacity; no general reset/replay, device-reset recovery, bounded
recovery time, or availability guarantee is provided. The journal is in-memory,
not durable ACID or arbitrary cross-rank atomic commit. Current Mixed
collectives default to host participation; neither typed device permits nor
NCCL plugin initialization proves a default device reduction or payload route.

The safe blocking reference is not implemented, and no physical-overlap
trace closes RQ3. Provider blocking, synchronization, cyclic resource waits,
and stalled progress remain possible. Asynchronous submission is not
system-wide nonblocking execution or lock freedom. The intended modality scope
extends beyond text, but other modalities and their quality oracles remain
future implementation work.

\paragraph{Comparisons and reproducibility.}
The two resident TP2 crossblocks show tradeoffs, not a fair, causal, or
statistically confirmed speedup: arithmetic and budgets differ, and the
number of independent blocks is small. Startup-metadata observations use
uncontrolled caches and unpaired historical context. Local artifacts require
portable archival, source/binary/model identities, and deterministic tables
and plots before publication. Closing a quality, traffic, or custody gate
in one profile does not close it in another.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\system{} connects storage-backed inference to an explicit execution contract:
freeze a source-bound plan, admit covered claims, materialize exact
generations, publish only validated bindings, and retain custody until the
relevant physical references end. The physical/accounting distinction makes
capacity measurable; the normal walkthrough and structured algorithms make
readiness, publication, and reuse separate testable transitions.

The current result is narrower than that target. One TP1 DEBUG run records
four HTTP successes, three limited reference canaries, repeated
materialization counters, and sampled memory. Physical storage traffic,
complete TP1 numerical quality, capacity necessity, a same-contract scheduling
comparison, and final custody closure remain open. Separate TP2 numerical and
resident timing evidence is useful context but does not fill these gates.
The paper's next evidentiary step is a profile-bound complete-output capacity
study with attributed working sets, aligned physical I/O, safe blocking/async
controls, and integrated fault-ledger observations. Until then, the design is
not a demonstrated beyond-memory result or a general performance/quality
claim.
