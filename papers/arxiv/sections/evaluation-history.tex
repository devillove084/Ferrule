\subsection{Numerical coverage}
For each retained-fresh and ordinary schedule in
Table~\ref{tab:enabled-quality}, common-history and free-feedback streams each
cover 27 calls and 9,187,840 vocabulary values; reset covers nine calls and
3,228,160 values, and fork covers one call and 248,320 values. For each
cold-owner schedule, common history, feedback, and reset each cover nine
calls and 3,228,160 values; fork covers one call and 248,320 values.
Fingerprints identify compared outputs rather than adding logit observations.
Ordinary execution does not observe diagnostic route or state fields.
The fork check replays a same-history committed frontier; it does not test
divergent KV copy-on-write or broad task accuracy.

The pressure study supplies narrower evidence at its three new cache points:
complete visible outputs and actual generated IDs. It does not establish
full-vocabulary, route, or internal-state equivalence at those settings.
Likewise, the tiny component fixture tests lifecycle behavior, not the
numerical quality of the complete 35B model.

\subsection{Earlier logical-demand observation}
\label{sec:tp1-storage-middle}
A separate, heavily instrumented single-lifecycle observation with the read
pipeline disabled completed four serial requests. One was a two-token
length/usage smoke; three passed visible-text and eight-token usage canaries.
Generated IDs, logits, routes, and states were not observed. Its 30 decoder
invocations recorded 36,240,064,128 logical reader bytes, 36,240,248,688
upload-interface bytes, 11,519 expert uploads, 11,455 evictions, and zero
expert hits. The final observed cache held 64 entries. These values describe
an earlier logical-demand workload, not additional repetitions of either
schedule in the paired experiment.

At the observed invocation boundaries, entry counts reconcile as
\begin{equation}
 N_j=N_{j-1}+I_j-E_j,
 \label{eq:entry-reconciliation}
\end{equation}
where $N_j$ is resident entries and $I_j,E_j$ are installation and eviction
deltas. The initial zero is implied by the first boundary, not directly
measured. This count identity does not establish byte conservation or
continuous occupancy. Historical plots and the detailed timing and memory
samples remain in the supplementary materials rather than extending the
current performance evidence.

\subsection{Unmatched resident-system supplement}
\label{sec:resident-supplement}
Two separate resident tensor-parallel blocks compared \system{} with vLLM
using fresh owners, three excluded QA requests, and five measured requests
per window. The observed tradeoff differs by metric: \system{} has higher
output rate and lower mean end-to-end latency in both windows, while vLLM has
lower TTFT, startup time, sampled device memory, and host PSS
(Table~\ref{tab:resident-crossblocks}).

Both selected deployments use pipeline parallelism one and tensor parallelism
two, but their arithmetic differs: \system{} uses its qualified Mixed
GemmEx/routing path, while vLLM uses BF16/FP8 Marlin execution. A 22 GiB
per-device policy and a device-utilization fraction of 0.8 also do not match
total working-set budgets. The descriptive output-rate ratios of 1.380 and
1.433 consequently do not establish a causal speedup or a state-of-the-art
ranking. The measured vLLM version is 0.28.0 with CUDA 12.9 and Torch 2.13.0.
Unpaired startup-metadata observations and further numerical comparisons are
retained with the artifacts and are not combined with these windows.

The independent W8A16 strict numerical comparison fails logits, ordered
routes, and state checks despite argmax agreement. That negative result
remains a failure of its own quality contract and cannot be replaced by the
successful Mixed-profile observations.
