\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Storage-backed inference repeatedly brings selected weights into an execution
cache rather than keeping every execution weight resident. This approach can
reduce device residency, but a small cache alone does not bound the resources
used to fill it. A read may retain host buffers, an upload may outlive its
caller, and an evicted binding may still have device consumers. Releasing a
logical request therefore does not necessarily make its capacity reusable.
The central problem is to coordinate materialization and physical lifetime
under explicit resource limits.

Weight streaming and prefetch are established techniques in systems such as
FlexGen, ZeRO-Inference, and PowerInfer-2~\cite{sheng2023flexgen,deepspeed2022zeroinference,xue2024powerinfer2}.
\system{} studies a complementary question: how can a bounded materialization
schedule preserve source identity, accounting, and safe reuse across normal
completion, cancellation, and uncertain completion? We separate three
properties that are often conflated: a configured resource budget, a measured
physical working set, and evidence that a particular operation has finished
using its resources.

The design follows \emph{plan, admit, materialize, publish, execute, retire}.
A frozen plan identifies the source and destination generations. Admission
acquires the claims required by each stage. Completion validates the exact
operation before publication, while the physical owner retains buffers and
charges until the relevant consumers finish. If completion cannot be proved,
the owner retains the uncertain resources instead of treating an error or
cancellation as permission to reuse them.

The concrete scheduling mechanism is deliberately small. After uploading the
current routed expert, the operator can submit one read for the next selected
expert before submitting current computation. The blocking control uses the
same read worker but waits immediately at demand. Neither schedule changes
expert order, arithmetic, cache policy, or the synchronous forward interface.
This comparison isolates a scheduling choice without weakening the resource
or completion contract.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A resource and ownership model that distinguishes logical admission
  limits from physical memory, and producer completion from consumer retirement.
  \item Reference algorithms for exact materialization and bounded cleanup,
  together with an implemented, bounded next-expert schedule for a private
  model-operator path.
  \item An evaluation connecting output checks, resource accounting, logical
  I/O, client outcomes, correlated profiling, and scoped failure behavior.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Research questions.}
\begin{description}
  \item[RQ1: Bounded completion.] Can requests complete under the declared
  limits, and how does residency change across coupled cache budgets?
  \item[RQ2: I/O demand.] What work reaches the logical reader and shared
  filesystem client, and which physical traffic remains unknown?
  \item[RQ3: Scheduling.] Does bounded lookahead reduce waiting and client
  latency, and what read--compute overlap is directly observed?
  \item[RQ4: Failure and custody.] What resources and state remain after
  clean abort, uncertain completion, and HTTP cancellation?
\end{description}

For the evaluated single-device text profile, five paired blocks show 8.18\%
lower client end-to-end latency, 11.24\% lower time to first content, and
10.91\% higher qualified serial-window goodput with lookahead. All blocks
show lower host demand waiting, with equal logical reader work and no wasted
read bytes. These results concern one configuration and short fixed outputs;
they establish neither statistical significance nor performance leadership.
